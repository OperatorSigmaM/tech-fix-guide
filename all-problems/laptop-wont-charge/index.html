Laptop Won't Charge? 14 Ways to Fix "Plugged In Not Charging"

📅 Updated: January 2025 ⏱️ 17 min read Easy to Medium

🎯 Quick Summary

Main causes: Faulty adapter/cable (35%), charging port damaged (25%), battery dead (20%), BIOS settings (10%), software issue (10%)

Quick fixes: Try different outlet, check cable for damage, power cycle laptop, update BIOS, replace adapter

Success rate: 70% fixable at home, 20% need adapter replacement, 10% require professional repair

First: Diagnose the Type of Problem

Different symptoms = different causes:

🔴 "Plugged in, not charging" message:

  • Battery detected but not accepting charge
  • Most common on Windows laptops (especially Dell, HP, Lenovo)
  • Causes: BIOS settings, battery driver issue, battery health poor

🔴 No charging indicator at all:

  • Adapter LED not lighting up
  • No charging light on laptop
  • Causes: Dead adapter, damaged cable, bad outlet, charging port broken

🔴 Charges very slowly:

  • Battery percentage increases but takes hours
  • Causes: Wrong wattage adapter, USB-C charging issue, battery degradation

🔴 Charges only when powered off:

  • Won't charge while laptop is on
  • Causes: Power-hungry software, insufficient adapter wattage, overheating

🔴 Charges intermittently:

  • Stops and starts charging randomly
  • Causes: Loose charging port, damaged cable, connection issue
💡 Quick check - Is it adapter or battery?
• If laptop runs on AC power without battery installed = adapter works, battery is problem
• If laptop won't run on AC power alone = adapter or charging port issue
• Check adapter LED - if no light = adapter dead or not getting power

Quick Fixes to Try First

1 Try Different Power Outlet Success: 15%

Why it works: Outlet might be dead, switched off, or have loose connection.

Check:

  1. Try different outlet in same room
  2. Try different room entirely
  3. Avoid power strips - plug directly into wall outlet
  4. If using power strip, test another device in it to confirm strip works
  5. Check if outlet has a switch (some do)
💡 Quick outlet test: Plug in phone charger or lamp to verify outlet has power before troubleshooting laptop further.

2 Inspect Adapter and Cable Success: 30%

Why it works: Adapters and cables fail more often than people think. Physical damage is common.

Check adapter brick:

  • LED indicator: Should be lit when plugged in. If dark = adapter dead
  • Temperature: Should be warm when working. If cold after 10 min = not working
  • Smell: Burning smell = internal failure
  • Physical damage: Cracks, dents, or warping

Check cable:

  • Fraying: Especially near connectors (most common failure point)
  • Kinks/bends: Sharp bends damage internal wires
  • Exposed wires: Obvious sign of damage
  • Connector damage: Bent pins, corrosion, loose fit

Test cable continuity:

  1. Wiggle cable at both ends while plugged in
  2. If charging light flickers = cable damaged internally
  3. Bend cable gently at different points
  4. If charging starts when bent = broken wire inside
⚠️ Do NOT use damaged cables: Exposed wires can cause electric shock, fire, or damage laptop. Replace immediately.

3 Power Cycle Laptop Success: 20%

Why it works: Clears residual charge, resets charging circuit, fixes software glitches.

Proper power cycle method:

  1. Shut down laptop completely (not sleep/hibernate)
  2. Unplug AC adapter from laptop
  3. Remove battery (if removable)
  4. Hold power button for 30 seconds (drains residual power)
  5. Plug adapter back in (leave battery out)
  6. Turn on laptop
  7. If boots = adapter works. Shut down and reinstall battery
  8. Plug adapter in again and test charging

For non-removable battery laptops:

  1. Shut down completely
  2. Unplug adapter
  3. Hold power button 60 seconds
  4. Plug adapter back in
  5. Power on and check charging
✓ Expected result: Charging resumes normally. If not, continue to next fixes.

4 Check Charging Port for Damage/Debris Success: 15%

Why it works: Dust, lint, or physical damage prevents proper connection.

Inspect charging port:

  • Look inside with flashlight: Check for debris, bent pins, corrosion
  • Loose connection: Plug wiggles excessively = worn port
  • Broken plastic: Housing cracked or separated
  • Push feeling: Should click firmly into place, not feel mushy

Safe cleaning method:

  1. Power off laptop and unplug
  2. Use wooden toothpick or plastic dental pick
  3. Gently remove visible debris
  4. Use compressed air (short bursts, can upright)
  5. Do NOT use metal objects (can short circuit or bend pins)
  6. Do NOT use vacuum (creates static)

Test fit:

  1. Plug adapter in carefully
  2. Should slide in smoothly and feel secure
  3. Gently wiggle - should not move excessively
  4. If very loose or falls out = port damaged, needs repair

5 Cool Down Overheated Laptop Success: 10%

Why it works: Some laptops stop charging when overheated as safety mechanism.

Signs laptop is overheated:

  • Bottom is very hot to touch
  • Fans running at maximum speed constantly
  • Performance is sluggish
  • Started after gaming or video editing

Cool down method:

  1. Shut down laptop (not sleep)
  2. Unplug adapter
  3. Place on hard, flat surface in cool room
  4. Wait 30 minutes minimum
  5. Clean vents with compressed air
  6. Plug adapter back in and test

6 Verify Adapter Wattage Success: 10%

Why it matters: Using wrong wattage adapter causes slow charging or no charging.

Check your adapter:

  1. Look at adapter label for "Output" or "Power"
  2. Should say something like "65W", "90W", "130W"
  3. Compare to laptop's requirements (check bottom sticker or manual)

Common wattage by laptop type:

  • Ultrabooks/13" laptops: 45-65W
  • 15" standard laptops: 65-90W
  • Gaming laptops: 120-240W
  • MacBook Air: 30-45W
  • MacBook Pro 16": 96-140W
⚠️ Using lower wattage adapter:
• Laptop may charge slowly or not at all while in use
• Might only charge when laptop is off
• Can damage battery over time
• Find correct wattage adapter for your model

USB-C charging special case:

  • Not all USB-C chargers support laptop charging
  • Need USB-C with "Power Delivery" (PD)
  • Minimum 45W for most laptops (check specs)
  • Phone chargers (5-20W) won't charge laptop

7 Update Battery Drivers (Windows) Success: 15%

Why it works: Corrupted battery driver causes "plugged in, not charging" error.

Method 1 - Uninstall and reinstall:

  1. Right-click Start menu → Device Manager
  2. Expand "Batteries"
  3. Right-click "Microsoft ACPI-Compliant Control Method Battery"
  4. Select "Uninstall device"
  5. Check "Delete the driver software" if option appears
  6. Click Uninstall
  7. Repeat for "Microsoft AC Adapter" if present
  8. Restart laptop
  9. Windows will reinstall drivers automatically

Method 2 - Update driver:

  1. Device Manager → Batteries
  2. Right-click battery driver
  3. Update driver
  4. Search automatically for drivers
  5. Restart after update

Software Solutions

8 Reset/Update BIOS Success: 20%

Why it works: BIOS controls charging circuitry. Corrupted settings or outdated BIOS can prevent charging.

Method 1 - Reset BIOS to defaults:

  1. Restart laptop
  2. Press BIOS key repeatedly during boot (usually F2, F10, Del, or Esc)
  3. Look for "Load Defaults", "Load Setup Defaults", or "Reset to Default"
  4. Confirm and save
  5. Exit BIOS (laptop will restart)
  6. Check if charging works

Method 2 - Update BIOS:

  1. Go to laptop manufacturer's support website
  2. Enter your model number
  3. Download latest BIOS update
  4. Follow manufacturer's instructions carefully
  5. IMPORTANT: Keep laptop plugged in during BIOS update
  6. CRITICAL: Do NOT interrupt update (can brick laptop)
🚨 BIOS update warning: If update fails or is interrupted, laptop may not boot. Only update BIOS if you're comfortable or have tried everything else first.

Common BIOS keys by manufacturer:

  • Dell: F2 or F12
  • HP: F10 or Esc
  • Lenovo: F2, F1, or Fn+F2
  • ASUS: F2 or Del
  • Acer: F2 or Del
  • MSI: Del

9 Run Windows Battery Report Diagnostic

Why it helps: Shows battery health and identifies if battery is the problem.

Generate battery report:

  1. Right-click Start menu → Windows Terminal (Admin) or Command Prompt (Admin)
  2. Type: powercfg /batteryreport
  3. Press Enter
  4. Report saved to: C:\Windows\System32\battery-report.html
  5. Open File Explorer, navigate to that location
  6. Double-click file to open in browser

Key things to check in report:

  • Design Capacity vs Full Charge Capacity: If Full Charge is under 40% of Design = battery worn out
  • Cycle Count: Most batteries last 300-500 cycles. Over 500 = likely time for replacement
  • Recent Usage: Shows if battery is charging at all
  • Battery Usage: Abnormal discharge rate indicates problem
💡 Example: Design Capacity: 50,000 mWh, Full Charge Capacity: 15,000 mWh = Battery only holds 30% of original capacity. Needs replacement.

10 Disable Battery Charge Threshold (If Enabled) Success: 10%

Why it works: Some laptops have settings that stop charging at 80% to extend battery life. Might be enabled without you knowing.

Check manufacturer software:

  • Dell: Dell Power Manager → "Battery" → Check if charging threshold enabled
  • HP: HP Support Assistant → Battery Health Manager
  • Lenovo: Lenovo Vantage → Power → Battery Charge Threshold
  • ASUS: MyASUS → Battery Health Charging
  • MSI: Dragon Center → Battery Master

What to do:

  1. Open manufacturer's utility app
  2. Find battery or power settings
  3. Look for "Conservation Mode", "Battery Lifespan", "Charge Threshold"
  4. Disable or set to 100%
  5. Restart and test charging

In BIOS:

  1. Some laptops have this setting in BIOS
  2. Look for "Battery Care", "Battery Conservation", or "Charge Control"
  3. Set to "Fully Charged" or "100%"

11 Reset SMC (Mac) Success: 25%

Why it works: SMC (System Management Controller) controls charging on Mac. Resetting fixes many charging issues.

MacBook with T2 chip (2018-2020):

  1. Shut down Mac
  2. Hold Shift + Control + Option (left side) + Power button for 10 seconds
  3. Release all keys
  4. Power on normally

MacBook with Apple Silicon (M1/M2/M3):

  1. Shut down Mac
  2. Wait 30 seconds
  3. Power on (SMC resets automatically on Apple Silicon)

Older MacBook (pre-2018):

  1. Shut down Mac
  2. Hold Shift + Control + Option + Power button for 10 seconds
  3. Release all keys
  4. Power on
💡 After SMC reset: Fan behavior, battery charging, display brightness may behave differently for a few minutes as system recalibrates.

Hardware Fixes & Replacements

12 Replace Adapter/Cable Cost: $20-80

When to replace: Adapter LED doesn't light, cable frayed, wiggling cable makes charging work intermittently.

Buying replacement adapter:

  • Best option: OEM (original manufacturer) adapter from official store
  • Good alternative: Certified third-party (look for UL/CE certification)
  • Avoid: Cheap generic Amazon/eBay adapters (fire hazard, can damage laptop)

What to check before buying:

  1. Wattage: Match or exceed original (check laptop bottom sticker)
  2. Voltage: Must match exactly (usually 19V or 20V)
  3. Connector type: Barrel plug size or USB-C
  4. Polarity: Should match (usually center positive)

Where to buy:

  • Dell: Dell.com official parts store, $40-80
  • HP: HP Parts Store, $40-70
  • Lenovo: Lenovo Parts, $40-70
  • Apple: Apple Store, $79 (USB-C), $99 (MagSafe)
  • Third-party: Anker, Nekteck (quality brands), $30-60
⚠️ USB-C charging: Not all USB-C chargers work with all laptops. Must support "USB Power Delivery" and have sufficient wattage (usually 45W minimum).

13 Replace Battery Cost: $40-150

When to replace battery:

  • Battery report shows under 50% capacity remaining
  • Battery drains very quickly (under 2 hours)
  • Battery is 3+ years old
  • Windows shows "Consider replacing battery" message
  • Laptop works fine on AC power but battery doesn't charge

Battery replacement options:

User-replaceable batteries (older laptops):

  1. Buy replacement battery for your model
  2. Power off laptop and unplug
  3. Flip laptop over
  4. Slide battery release latch
  5. Remove old battery
  6. Insert new battery (align contacts)
  7. Should click into place
  8. Power on and charge to 100%

Internal batteries (most modern laptops):

  • DIY replacement: Requires opening laptop, disconnecting battery connector. Difficulty: Medium. Cost: $40-80 for battery
  • Professional replacement: Best option for beginners. Cost: $80-150 including labor
  • Manufacturer service: Most expensive but guaranteed. Cost: $150-250

Where to buy batteries:

  • OEM (best): Manufacturer's parts store
  • iFixit (good): Quality third-party batteries with tools and guides
  • Amazon (careful): Check reviews, avoid no-name brands
  • eBay (risky): Often counterfeit or used batteries
🚨 Swollen battery warning: If battery is swollen (laptop won't sit flat, trackpad lifted, bulging), STOP USING IMMEDIATELY. Don't charge. Take to professional. Fire/explosion risk.

14 Repair/Replace Charging Port Cost: $80-200

When charging port needs repair:

  • Plug falls out easily or feels very loose
  • Have to hold plug at specific angle to charge
  • Charging stops when you move laptop
  • Physical damage visible (cracked housing, bent pins)
  • Tried multiple adapters and all have same issue

Repair options:

  • Professional repair: $80-150 for most laptops
  • Manufacturer service: $150-250
  • DIY repair: Very difficult, requires soldering. Only for experienced. Parts: $10-30

Temporary workaround (until repair):

  • Find angle that works and secure with tape
  • Prop laptop against something to maintain angle
  • Don't move laptop while charging
  • Charge when laptop is off (less stress on port)
💡 USB-C laptops: If your laptop has multiple USB-C ports, try charging from different port. One port might be damaged while others work.

How to Prevent Charging Problems

1. Proper Adapter Care

  • Don't wrap cable tightly around adapter: Creates kinks that break internal wires
  • Leave slack near connectors: Most stress happens within 2 inches of plug
  • Store properly: Loose coil, not tight wrap
  • Keep adapter cool: Don't cover with blankets or bury under pillows
  • Avoid pulling by cable: Always pull plug, not cable

2. Charging Port Protection

  • Insert gently: Don't force if it doesn't slide in smoothly
  • Remove straight out: Don't wiggle or angle while removing
  • Support cable: Don't let cable hang freely pulling on port
  • Keep clean: Blow out dust monthly with compressed air
  • Avoid side pressure: Don't step on cable or let it get pulled sideways

3. Battery Health Maintenance

  • Avoid extreme temperatures: Don't use laptop in hot car or freezing cold
  • Partial charges are better: 20-80% range is healthier than 0-100% repeatedly
  • Don't leave plugged in 24/7: Use battery occasionally (once a week minimum)
  • Store at 50% if unused: Long-term storage at full or empty damages battery
  • Update BIOS: Newer BIOS versions have better battery management

4. Use Manufacturer Tools

  • Enable battery conservation mode: If laptop mostly stays plugged in
  • Use adaptive charging: MacOS has optimized battery charging
  • Check battery health regularly: Monthly via battery report or system tools

When to Replace vs Repair

✓ Worth repairing if:

  • Laptop is under 3 years old
  • High-end or gaming laptop ($1000+)
  • Only issue is charging (everything else works great)
  • Repair cost under $200
  • Has warranty or insurance

⚠️ Consider replacing laptop if:

  • Laptop is 5+ years old
  • Budget laptop (under $500 original price)
  • Multiple problems (slow, overheating, damaged screen)
  • Repair cost exceeds 50% of replacement cost
  • Battery + charging port both need replacement ($200-300)

Common Questions

Q: Laptop says "plugged in, not charging." What does this mean?

A: Adapter is working (laptop recognizes it) but battery isn't accepting charge. Usually: battery driver issue (try reinstall), BIOS setting, or battery worn out (check battery report).

Q: Can I use any USB-C charger to charge my laptop?

A: No. Must support "USB Power Delivery" (PD) and have sufficient wattage. Phone chargers (5-20W) won't work. Need minimum 45W for most laptops, 65W+ for larger laptops.

Q: My adapter works fine with laptop powered off but not when it's on. Why?

A: Adapter wattage is insufficient for your laptop's power draw when running. Get correct wattage adapter for your model.

Q: How do I know if my adapter or battery is the problem?

A: Remove battery (if removable). If laptop runs on AC power alone = adapter works, battery is problem. If doesn't run on AC alone = adapter or charging port issue.

Q: Is it safe to use laptop with dead battery on AC power only?

A: Yes, perfectly safe. Many people do this. Just means instant shutdown if power is interrupted (no battery backup).

Q: Can I replace laptop battery myself?

A: Depends on model. Older laptops have user-replaceable batteries (easy). Modern laptops require opening case (medium difficulty). MacBooks are difficult (professional recommended).
