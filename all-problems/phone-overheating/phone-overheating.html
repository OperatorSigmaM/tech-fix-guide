Phone Overheating? 14 Ways to Cool It Down Fast

📅 Updated: January 2025 ⏱️ 16 min read Easy

🎯 Quick Summary

Main causes: Heavy app usage (30%), direct sunlight (25%), charging while using (20%), background processes (15%), hardware defect (10%)

Quick fixes: Close apps, remove case, avoid sunlight, stop charging while using, enable airplane mode temporarily

Normal temps: 32-35°C (89-95°F) idle, 35-40°C (95-104°F) in use. Above 45°C (113°F) = problem

Normal vs Dangerous Phone Temperatures

✓ Normal (32-40°C / 89-104°F): Warm to touch during use

⚠️ Warm (40-45°C / 104-113°F): Noticeably hot, take action

🚨 Too Hot (45°C+ / 113°F+): Uncomfortable to hold, risk of damage

💡 How to check temperature:
• iPhone: No built-in temp display. If you see "Temperature warning" screen = too hot
• Android: Download "CPU-Z" or "AIDA64" app to see exact temperature
• Manual test: If too hot to comfortably hold for 10 seconds = problem

What Causes Phone Overheating

Environmental causes:

  • Direct sunlight: Fastest way to overheat, can reach 50°C+ in minutes
  • Hot car: Interior temps can hit 70°C (158°F) in summer
  • Charging in hot room: Charging generates heat + ambient heat = overload
  • Poor ventilation: Phone in tight pocket, under pillow, in closed case

Usage causes:

  • Gaming: Especially graphics-intensive games (Genshin Impact, PUBG, COD)
  • Video recording: 4K video recording heats CPU and GPU intensely
  • GPS navigation: Screen on + GPS + data connection = heat buildup
  • Video calls: Camera + screen + processing = sustained heat
  • Charging + using: Generates extra heat from charging circuit

Software causes:

  • Background apps: Apps running even when not visible
  • Malware/virus: Malicious apps use CPU constantly
  • Software updates: Installing or indexing uses heavy processing
  • Sync services: iCloud, Google Photos uploading constantly

Hardware causes:

  • Battery degradation: Old batteries heat up more when charging
  • Defective battery: Can swell and overheat (fire hazard)
  • Charging port damage: Poor connection causes resistance and heat
  • Processor defect: Rare but can cause chronic overheating
🚨 Danger signs - Stop using immediately:
• Phone is painfully hot to touch
• Battery swelling (back cover bulging)
• Burning smell
• Unexpected shutdowns from heat
• Screen showing temperature warning
→ Power off, let cool, take to professional

Immediate Solutions to Cool Phone Down

1 Stop Using and Remove Case Impact: High

Why it works: Reduces heat generation and improves heat dissipation.

Steps:

  1. Close all apps (swipe up on iPhone, Recent Apps button on Android)
  2. Remove phone case immediately (cases trap heat)
  3. Place on cool, flat surface
  4. Leave alone for 10-15 minutes
✓ Expected result: Temperature drops 5-10°C in 15 minutes.

2 Turn Off Phone (If Very Hot) Impact: Highest

Why it works: Stops all CPU activity immediately, allows fastest cooling.

When to do this:

  • Phone showing temperature warning
  • Too hot to comfortably hold
  • Has been hot for 20+ minutes

Steps:

  1. Power off phone completely
  2. Remove case
  3. Place in cool (not cold), well-ventilated area
  4. Wait 30-45 minutes before turning back on
⚠️ DO NOT:
• Put in refrigerator/freezer (condensation damages electronics)
• Place in front of AC vent (too sudden cooling causes condensation)
• Put under running water (even if water resistant)

3 Unplug from Charger Impact: High

Why it works: Charging generates significant heat. Using phone while charging compounds the problem.

Charging heat facts:

  • Fast charging (18W+) generates more heat than standard charging (5W)
  • Wireless charging generates 20-30% more heat than wired
  • Using phone while charging can increase temps by 10-15°C

If phone is hot while charging:

  1. Unplug immediately
  2. Let cool for 15 minutes
  3. Resume charging when cool
  4. Don't use phone while charging

4 Enable Airplane Mode Impact: Medium

Why it works: Disables cellular, WiFi, Bluetooth radios which consume power and generate heat searching for signals.

When useful:

  • In area with weak signal (phone uses more power trying to connect)
  • Not currently needing connectivity
  • Want to cool phone quickly without turning off

How to enable:

  • iPhone: Settings → Airplane Mode ON, or Control Center
  • Android: Settings → Network → Airplane Mode, or Quick Settings

Leave in airplane mode for 10-15 minutes while phone cools.

5 Lower Screen Brightness Impact: Low-Medium

Why it works: Screen is one of largest power consumers. Lower brightness = less heat.

Quick brightness reduction:

  • iPhone: Control Center → Drag brightness slider down
  • Android: Quick Settings → Drag brightness slider down

Set to 50% or lower when phone is overheating.

6 Move to Cool Location Impact: High

Common overheating locations to avoid:

  • Car dashboard: Can reach 70°C (158°F) in sunlight
  • Direct sunlight: Even indoors through window
  • Near heat sources: Stoves, heaters, laptops
  • Tight pockets: Body heat + no ventilation

Best cooling locations:

  • Shaded, air-conditioned room
  • Near (not in front of) fan
  • Cool desk or table surface
  • Well-ventilated area

7 Force Close Background Apps Impact: Medium

Why it works: Apps running in background use CPU and generate heat.

iPhone:

  1. Swipe up from bottom (or double-click Home button on older iPhones)
  2. Swipe up on each app preview to close
  3. Close all apps except essentials

Android:

  1. Tap Recent Apps button (square icon)
  2. Swipe away all app cards
  3. Or tap "Close All" button

Apps that often cause overheating:

  • Facebook, Instagram (constantly refreshing)
  • Streaming apps (Netflix, YouTube running in background)
  • Games (even when "closed" might still run)
  • Photo apps (processing/uploading in background)

8 Turn Off Location Services Temporarily Impact: Medium

Why it works: GPS is power-hungry, generates significant heat.

iPhone:

  1. Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services
  2. Toggle OFF temporarily
  3. Or set apps to "While Using" instead of "Always"

Android:

  1. Settings → Location
  2. Toggle OFF
  3. Or Quick Settings → Tap Location icon

Re-enable when phone has cooled and you need GPS again.

Long-Term Fixes for Chronic Overheating

9 Update Software Impact: Medium

Why it works: Updates include bug fixes, power management improvements, and thermal optimizations.

iPhone:

  1. Settings → General → Software Update
  2. Download and Install if available
  3. Connect to WiFi and charger during update
  4. Do this when phone is cool (not overheating)

Android:

  1. Settings → System → System Update (location varies by manufacturer)
  2. Check for updates
  3. Install if available
💡 Note: Phone may run warm for 24-48 hours after major update while re-indexing and optimizing. This is normal.

10 Disable Auto-Brightness (Temporarily) Impact: Low

Why it works: Auto-brightness sensor constantly adjusts, using power. Manual control can reduce heat.

iPhone:

  1. Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size
  2. Turn OFF "Auto-Brightness"
  3. Manually set to 40-50%

Android:

  1. Settings → Display → Adaptive Brightness
  2. Toggle OFF
  3. Manually adjust to comfortable level

11 Check Battery Health Impact: Diagnostic

Why it matters: Degraded batteries overheat more easily and can be dangerous.

iPhone battery health check:

  1. Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging
  2. Check "Maximum Capacity"
  3. Below 80% = battery replacement recommended
  4. Look for "Service" message

Android battery health check:

  1. Download "AccuBattery" app (free, accurate)
  2. Use phone normally for 3-5 days
  3. Check "Health" tab for battery capacity
  4. Below 80% = consider replacement

Signs battery needs replacement:

  • Phone gets hot even during light use
  • Battery drains very quickly
  • Phone shuts down unexpectedly
  • Back cover is slightly bulging
  • Charging very slow or won't charge
🚨 Swollen battery = immediate danger: If back cover is bulging, stop using immediately. Do NOT charge. Battery can catch fire or explode. Take to professional same day.

12 Factory Reset (Last Resort) Success: 40%

Why it works: Removes software bugs, malware, corrupted files causing overheating.

When to try:

  • Phone constantly overheats even during idle
  • Recently installed app caused problem but can't identify which
  • All other software fixes didn't work
  • Phone is under 2 years old (hardware likely fine)

⚠️ BACKUP EVERYTHING FIRST - This erases all data!

iPhone factory reset:

  1. Backup: Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → iCloud Backup → Back Up Now
  2. Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone
  3. Erase All Content and Settings
  4. Enter passcode and confirm
  5. Wait 30-60 minutes
  6. Set up as new (don't restore backup yet to test)

Android factory reset:

  1. Backup: Settings → Google → Backup (ensure recent backup exists)
  2. Settings → System → Reset Options
  3. Erase all data (factory reset)
  4. Confirm and enter PIN/password
  5. Wait 30 minutes
✓ Testing after reset: Use phone for 2-3 days WITHOUT restoring backup or installing many apps. If overheating stops = software issue confirmed. Restore selectively.

13 Identify Problematic Apps Impact: High

Why it works: One bad app can cause chronic overheating.

iPhone - Check battery usage:

  1. Settings → Battery
  2. Scroll down to "Battery Usage by App"
  3. Look for apps using excessive battery (especially in background)
  4. Tap app → Battery Usage → Disable "Background App Refresh"
  5. Or uninstall if not essential

Android - Check battery usage:

  1. Settings → Battery → Battery Usage
  2. See which apps consume most power
  3. Tap app → Restrict background activity
  4. Or Force Stop / Uninstall

Common culprit apps:

  • Facebook/Instagram: Constant refresh, video autoplay
  • Snapchat: Camera/GPS constantly running
  • Games: Especially if notifications enabled (wakes phone frequently)
  • Cryptocurrency apps: Some mine in background
  • VPN apps: If left running constantly
  • Weather apps: Constant location tracking

14 Replace Battery or Phone Cost: $50-$500

Battery replacement when:

  • Battery health below 80%
  • Phone is 2-3 years old
  • Overheating started after battery degradation
  • Phone otherwise works fine

Battery replacement costs:

  • iPhone: $69-89 at Apple Store
  • Samsung: $50-80 at authorized service
  • Third-party: $40-60 (quality varies)
  • DIY: $20-30 for battery + tools (voids warranty)

Replace entire phone when:

  • Phone is 4+ years old
  • Overheating is due to processor/motherboard issue
  • Repair cost exceeds 50% of replacement cost
  • Multiple hardware issues present

Prevention: Stop Overheating Before It Starts

1. Charging Best Practices

  • Don't use phone while charging - Biggest cause of charging heat
  • Charge in cool location - Not on bed, pillow, or in sunlight
  • Remove case when charging - Especially thick cases
  • Use original charger - Or certified alternatives (Apple MFi, USB-IF certified)
  • Avoid overnight charging - Or use "Optimized Battery Charging" (keeps at 80% until morning)
  • Unplug at 80-90% - Don't need to charge to 100% every time

2. Usage Habits

  • Close unused apps - Don't leave 50 apps open
  • Limit gaming sessions - Take 10-minute break every 30 minutes during intensive gaming
  • Lower graphics in games - Medium settings generate less heat than Ultra
  • Use WiFi over cellular - When possible, WiFi uses less power
  • Download content before trips - Streaming uses more power than local playback
  • Turn off features when not needed - Bluetooth, WiFi, GPS, NFC

3. Environmental Awareness

  • Never leave in hot car - Even for "just 5 minutes"
  • Avoid direct sunlight - Even indoors through window
  • Keep out of tight pockets - Especially during exercise
  • Don't use in saunas/hot tubs - Obviously, but people do it
  • Store in cool, dry place - Not near radiators, ovens, etc.

4. Software Maintenance

  • Keep OS updated - Updates include thermal improvements
  • Update apps regularly - App updates fix bugs that cause overheating
  • Review app permissions - Revoke unnecessary location/background access
  • Disable auto-sync for non-essential apps - Constant syncing = constant power use
  • Use Low Power Mode - When battery is low or phone is warm (reduces performance but also heat)

5. Case Selection

  • Choose breathable cases - Cases with ventilation or cooling features
  • Avoid thick rubber cases - Trap heat effectively
  • Remove case during intensive tasks - Gaming, video recording, charging
  • Consider no case at home - Use case only when going out

When Overheating is Normal vs Problematic

✓ Normal/Expected Overheating:

  • Gaming for 30+ minutes (especially intensive games)
  • 4K video recording
  • GPS navigation in hot weather
  • Fast charging (18W+)
  • First 24 hours after major OS update
  • Initial setup of new phone
  • Large file download/upload

Action: Let cool between sessions, otherwise normal operation.

🚨 Problematic Overheating:

  • Gets hot during idle/light use (browsing, texting)
  • Hot even when not using phone
  • Painful to touch
  • Battery swelling
  • Unexpected shutdowns
  • Started suddenly after drop or water exposure
  • Gets hot in specific area (near camera, bottom edge)

Action: Investigate immediately, likely hardware issue requiring professional repair.

Summary: Quick Action Plan

Phone is Hot RIGHT NOW?

  1. Stop using immediately
  2. Close all apps
  3. Unplug from charger
  4. Remove case
  5. Enable airplane mode
  6. Place on cool surface
  7. Wait 15-20 minutes

If still hot after 20 minutes: Power off completely for 30 minutes.

Phone Constantly Overheats?

  1. Check battery health (Settings → Battery)
  2. Identify power-hungry apps and restrict/uninstall
  3. Update iOS/Android to latest version
  4. Disable unnecessary background services
  5. If battery health below 80% → Replace battery
  6. If phone is 3+ years old → Consider replacement
  7. If none work → Factory reset (backup first!)

Long-Term Effects of Overheating

Repeated or prolonged overheating damages:

  • Battery: Accelerates degradation, reduces lifespan by 30-50%
  • Processor: Thermal throttling = performance decreases over time
  • Display: Heat can cause screen discoloration, burn-in
  • Internal components: Solder joints can weaken, causing failures
  • Water resistance: Heat degrades adhesive seals
✓ Good news: Occasional overheating during intensive use is normal and won't cause damage. It's chronic, daily overheating during light use that causes problems.

Common Questions

Q: Can I cool my phone in the fridge?

A: NO. Rapid temperature change causes condensation inside phone, damaging electronics. Let it cool naturally at room temperature.

Q: Is it safe to use phone when it's warm?

A: Yes if just warm to touch. No if uncomfortable to hold. Phone has thermal protection and will shut down if dangerously hot.

Q: Will a cooling fan case help?

A: Yes for gaming. Cooling cases with active fans can reduce temps by 5-10°C during intensive use. Worth it for heavy gamers.

Q: My phone gets hot when charging overnight. Is this bad?

A: Mild warmth is normal. If HOT, stop overnight charging. Use "Optimized Battery Charging" (iPhone) or "Adaptive Charging" (Android) to reduce heat.

Q: Does closing apps actually help?

A: Yes for overheating. Apple says don't close apps for battery life, but for cooling it does help by reducing CPU load immediately.
